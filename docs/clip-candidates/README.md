# Demo clip candidates (RipVIS validation split)

Shortlist for `clip1_obvious` and `clip2_sneaky` (notes/contract.md). Generated by `ml/scripts/find_clips.py` from `val/coco_annotations/val.json` and `RipVISv1.8.4_dataset_info.pdf`. Only the **validation** split is used, because the model trained on train. Val videos 001, 007, 012, 014, 024 and 039 are excluded (their frame numbers do not match the video).

Each sheet shows 6 evenly spaced annotated frames, plain on the top row and with the ground-truth rip polygons in red on the bottom row. Times are seconds into the source video.

## Top picks

| Role | Pick | Window | Runner-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| clip1_obvious | **RipVIS-053** | 0-15 s | RipVIS-128 (1-16 s) |
| clip2_sneaky | **RipVIS-129** | 0-15 s | RipVIS-144 (0-13 s) |

**clip1_obvious: RipVIS-053.** It is the textbook rip: a calm, darker gap between breaking waves, filmed from a fixed elevated spot (a building or balcony) with beachgoers in frame.
- The camera does not move, so it reads as a real lifeguard tower feed on the dashboard, and the mask will not drift with camera motion.
- It has the steadiest annotation of all 24 videos (area CV 0.04) and one rip, about 10% of the frame.
- It also suits the "Where would you swim?" hook: the rip is the calm-looking water where an untrained swimmer would go in.
- Downside: 1280x720, so it looks a little soft full-screen on a 1080p recording.

Use RipVIS-128 if that is a problem, or if the model's mask on 053 is weak. 128 is 4K drone footage with the largest steady rip (18% of the frame), but the camera drifts and there are no people for scale.

**clip2_sneaky: RipVIS-129.** A postcard tropical beach seen from a fixed hillside spot: palms, blue sky, waves on rocks.
- The rip is a small (4.6% of the frame), brownish, slightly calmer channel in the middle distance. An untrained eye almost certainly misses it among the rocks and foam, which is what the /compare shot needs.
- The camera is static, the rip is a single instance, the annotation is steady (CV 0.13), and the clip is 68 s, so there is room to move the window.
- It is not a beach-level or phone view (none made the shortlist, see Notes). It is the closest thing to a calm, inviting scene.

Use RipVIS-144 if 129's rip turns out too small to read at dashboard tile size. 144 is a drone view of a kitesurfing beach with two rips that show as dark gaps in the whitewater.

## Candidates

Scores:
- **coverage**: annotated frames with a rip / annotated frames.
- **area**: mean rip area as a fraction of the frame, over rip frames.
- **CV**: coefficient of variation of that area across frames (lower is steadier).
- **inst**: rip instances per rip frame.
- **n**: annotated frames.

**RipVIS-128** ([sheet](clip1_obvious_1_RipVIS-128.jpg))
- **Scene:** sandy beach on the left, two bands of breaking whitewater, and a long band of deeper green water between them running along the shore and out. No people.
- **Visible to an untrained eye?** Partly. The calm green band stands out from the foam, but you need to be told it is a current. Once outlined it is obvious.
- **Window:** 1-16 s. The rip is steady throughout, and anything inside 0-20 s works.

**RipVIS-079** ([sheet](clip1_obvious_2_RipVIS-079.jpg))
- **Scene:** straight-down drone view with the shoreline along the bottom. Two brown sediment plumes push out through the surf into green water, the left one large.
- **Visible to an untrained eye?** Yes. The brown plumes are the most visible rips in the shortlist.
- **Downsides:** 720p, two instances, and the top-down view is abstract (hard to imagine swimming there).
- **Window:** 60-75 s. The left plume is largest and clearest from about 45 s on.

**RipVIS-053** ([sheet](clip1_obvious_3_RipVIS-053.jpg))
- **Scene:** fixed view from a building above a white-sand beach with people at the water's edge. Waves break on both sides of a calm, darker green gap, which is the rip.
- **Visible to an untrained eye?** Not as a danger. It looks like the calmest place to swim, which is the point of the hook. Once outlined, anyone gets it.
- **Window:** 0-15 s. The annotation barely changes (CV 0.04), and the whole 19 s is usable.

**RipVIS-143** ([sheet](clip1_obvious_5_RipVIS-143.jpg))
- **Scene:** drone flying along a kitesurfing beach. Wide whitewater with calm green patches (rips) between the breaking zones.
- **Visible to an untrained eye?** Moderately. The calm patches show, but the scene is busy.
- **Downsides:** after about 13 s the camera swings toward the shore and the rip splits into 2-3 pieces, hence CV 0.39.
- **Window:** 0-12 s (one rip, steadier camera). The script's 4-19 s suggestion runs into the messy part.

**RipVIS-147** ([sheet](clip2_sneaky_2_RipVIS-147.jpg))

- **Window:** 0-13 s (the whole clip, just over the 10 s minimum).

## Notes
- **Coverage is 1.00 for every val rip video:** every annotated frame has a rip, so it does not separate candidates. Ranking comes down to area, steadiness and instance count.
- **Viewpoint is judged by eye.** The dataset info PDF gives fps, duration, resolution, rip count and source, but no viewpoint, so the script cannot rank on it. Every shortlisted clip is drone or elevated; none is beach-level or phone footage. A beach-level sneaky shot, if one exists in val, is lower in the ranking. The next unseen candidates are RipVIS-134, 102, 046 and 044 (full tables: `ml/data/clip_scores.md` after running the script).
- **Ineligible:** videos under 10 s (015, 026, 059, 067, 072, 137) can't give a 10-20 s window. Under 720p (121, 480x360) the score takes a 0.6 penalty.
- **Ground truth lines up on all 8 sheets** (the red outline sits on the visible plume or gap), so frame numbers match the video for these clips.
- **4K picks:** 128, 129, 143, 144, 146 and 147 are 3840x2160. `infer_video.py` keeps the source resolution, so a 4K clip in the web dashboard (three videos playing at once) may not play smoothly. Downscaling the trimmed clip to 1920 wide would avoid that.
- **Sampling rates differ:** 128, 129, 144, 146 and 147 were annotated at 1 frame per second, so their stats rest on 14-69 frames. 053, 079 and 143 were annotated at 5 fps.
